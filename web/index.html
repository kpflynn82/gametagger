How it works

Describe first, then decide

GameTagger in 82 seconds

A silent film of the whole method, with this page's own numbers, then what comes next: play a game, learn what makes it work, and build a playable prototype.

  1. The NitroGen dataset's game list (40,000 hours of gameplay, 1,000+ games) seeded the catalog.
  2. 189 gameplay tags and 100 genres, each defined with the evidence allowed to prove it.
  3. Claude describes screenshots and video. Jev decides, one question per tag.
  4. Everything lands here.
  5. When evidence runs out, an AI player plays the game: 13 hours of a slingshot plane game, every forced ad counted.
  6. It works out what makes the game work, and what to keep, drop or replace.
  7. Then it builds a playable prototype, shown in real footage.

Claude describes

Claude looks at each screenshot and six short trailer clips and writes down what is visible: a first-person view of a rifle, a timer, a shop screen. It is not allowed to name genres or tags.

Jev decides

Jev, a classification model from TypeSafe, answers each of 189 tag questions on its own, using only the evidence each tag allows. It never sees the game's name, so it can't lean on reputation.

Unknown stays unknown

Every tag is present, absent, conflicting or not enough evidence, with a probability. A missing tag never quietly becomes a "no", and every tag keeps a record of the evidence behind it.
